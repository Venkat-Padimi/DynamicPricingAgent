# Mathematical & Deterministic Methodology Specification

This document details the exact mathematical formulas, statistical algorithms, appraisal adjustment rules, and scoring models implemented in the **Automated Valuation & Dynamic Pricing Agent**.

All calculations described herein are executed **100% deterministically** in pure Python outside the language model. No financial values, adjustments, or probability scores are generated by generative AI.

---

## 1. Multi-Attribute Comparable Similarity Scoring

### Purpose
Measures the dimensional, physical, and geographic resemblance between a candidate comparable transaction and the subject property on a normalized scale of `[0.0, 1.0]`.

### Mathematical Formulation
The composite similarity score $S$ is computed as the normalized weighted sum of seven sub-dimension similarities:

$$S = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{7} w_i \cdot s_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{7} w_i}$$

Where:
- $w_i \in \mathbb{R}^+$: Configurable dimensional weight
- $s_i \in [0.0, 1.0]$: Dimensional similarity index

### Dimensional Similarity Formulas

| Dimension ($i$) | Weight ($w_i$) | Formula ($s_i$) | Variable Definition | Deterministic? | Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Distance** | $0.25$ | $s_{\text{dist}} = 1.0 - \min\left(1.0, \frac{d}{d_{\max}}\right)$ | $d$: Haversine distance (mi); $d_{\max} = 3.0$ mi | Yes | Record coordinates vs Subject coordinates |
| **2. Square Footage** | $0.25$ | $s_{\text{sqft}} = 1.0 - \min\left(1.0, \frac{\|A_{\text{subj}} - A_{\text{comp}}\|}{A_{\text{subj}}}\right)$ | $A$: Gross living area in square feet | Yes | Record sqft vs Subject sqft |
| **3. Bedrooms** | $0.10$ | $s_{\text{bed}} = 1.0 - \min\left(1.0, \frac{\|B_{\text{subj}} - B_{\text{comp}}\|}{4.0}\right)$ | $B$: Bedroom count | Yes | Subject & comp bedroom count |
| **4. Bathrooms** | $0.10$ | $s_{\text{bath}} = 1.0 - \min\left(1.0, \frac{\|Ba_{\text{subj}} - Ba_{\text{comp}}\|}{3.0}\right)$ | $Ba$: Bathroom count | Yes | Subject & comp bathroom count |
| **5. Age / Vintage** | $0.10$ | $s_{\text{age}} = 1.0 - \min\left(1.0, \frac{\|Y_{\text{subj}} - Y_{\text{comp}}\|}{50.0}\right)$ | $Y$: Age in years ($2026 - \text{year built}$) | Yes | Subject & comp year built |
| **6. Condition** | $0.10$ | $s_{\text{cond}} = 1.0 - \frac{\|C_{\text{subj}} - C_{\text{comp}}\|}{4.0}$ | $C \in \{1: \text{Poor}, 2: \text{Fair}, 3: \text{Good}, 4: \text{Exc}, 5: \text{Lux}\}$ | Yes | Subject & comp condition enum |
| **7. Amenities** | $0.10$ | $s_{\text{amen}} = \frac{\|M_{\text{subj}} \cap M_{\text{comp}}\|}{\|M_{\text{subj}} \cup M_{\text{comp}}\|}$ | Jaccard similarity of amenity sets $M$ | Yes | Amenities string list |

**Total Default Weight Sum:** $\sum w_i = 1.00$.

## 2. Appraisal Feature Adjustments (CMA Engine)

### Core Appraisal Directionality Rule
Standard professional appraisal methodology is strictly enforced:
$$\text{The comparable property is adjusted to match the subject property.}$$

$$\text{AdjustedPrice}_{\text{comp}} = \text{SalePrice}_{\text{comp}} + \sum_{k=1}^{K} \text{Adj}_k$$

If the subject is superior to the comparable in a given attribute, the comparable receives a **positive adjustment** ($+$) to bring its value up to the subject. If the subject is inferior, the comparable receives a **negative adjustment** ($-$).

### Line-Item Adjustment Formulas

#### 1. Gross Living Area (GLA) Adjustment
$$\text{Adj}_{\text{sqft}} = (A_{\text{subj}} - A_{\text{comp}}) \times R_{\text{sqft}}$$
- $A_{\text{subj}}, A_{\text{comp}}$: Gross living area in square feet.
- $R_{\text{sqft}}$: Marginal sqft adjustment rate ($120.00 / sqft default).

#### 2. Bedroom Count Adjustment
$$\text{Adj}_{\text{bed}} = (B_{\text{subj}} - B_{\text{comp}}) \times \$15,000$$

#### 3. Bathroom Count Adjustment
$$\text{Adj}_{\text{bath}} = (Ba_{\text{subj}} - Ba_{\text{comp}}) \times \$10,000$$

#### 4. Property Age Adjustment
$$\text{Adj}_{\text{age}} = (\text{Age}_{\text{comp}} - \text{Age}_{\text{subj}}) \times \$1,000$$
*(Note: If the subject is younger than the comparable, $\text{Age}_{\text{comp}} - \text{Age}_{\text{subj}} > 0$, yielding a positive adjustment to the comp).*

#### 5. Property Condition Adjustment
$$\text{Adj}_{\text{cond}} = (C_{\text{subj}} - C_{\text{comp}}) \times \$12,500$$
- $C$: Condition rating mapped to ordinal values $1$ to $5$.

#### 6. Amenities Difference Adjustment
$$\text{Adj}_{\text{amen}} = (|M_{\text{subj}}| - |M_{\text{comp}}|) \times \$5,000$$
- Net count difference between subject and comparable premium amenities.

#### 7. Time / Market Appreciation Adjustment
$$\text{Adj}_{\text{time}} = \text{SalePrice}_{\text{comp}} \times \left( \frac{\text{DaysSinceSale}}{365.25} \times g_{\text{annual}} \right)$$
- $\text{DaysSinceSale}$: Days elapsed between comp closed sale date and evaluation date.
- $g_{\text{annual}}$: Annual sales price growth rate from submarket trend data.

## 3. Statistical Outlier Detection

To ensure extreme comps do not skew valuations, the CMA engine applies dual statistical tests on adjusted comparable prices.

### Method A: Interquartile Range (IQR) Test
1. Sort adjusted prices in ascending order: $P = [p_1, p_2, \dots, p_n]$.
2. Calculate the 25th percentile ($Q_1$) and 75th percentile ($Q_3$).
3. Compute Interquartile Range:
   $$\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$$
4. Establish outlier fences:
   $$\text{Lower Fence} = Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$$
   $$\text{Upper Fence} = Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$$
5. An adjusted price $p_k$ is flagged if $p_k < \text{Lower Fence}$ or $p_k > \text{Upper Fence}$.

### Method B: Z-Score Test
1. Compute sample mean ($\mu$) and standard deviation ($\sigma$):
   $$\mu = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n p_i, \quad \sigma = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^n (p_i - \mu)^2}$$
2. Compute standardized score for each comp:
   $$Z_k = \frac{|p_k - \mu|}{\sigma}$$
3. A comp is flagged as a statistical outlier if $Z_k > 2.0$.

## 4. Deterministic Multi-Component Valuation Engine

### Core Valuation Formula
The estimated market valuation $V_{\text{est}}$ is a convex combination of four distinct deterministic analytical components:

$$V_{\text{est}} = w_{\text{cma}} \cdot V_{\text{cma}} + w_{\text{trend}} \cdot V_{\text{trend}} + w_{\text{loc}} \cdot V_{\text{loc}} + w_{\text{yield}} \cdot V_{\text{yield}}$$

Where:
$$\sum w = w_{\text{cma}} + w_{\text{trend}} + w_{\text{loc}} + w_{\text{yield}} = 1.00$$

### Component Specifications & Default Weights

| Component | Default Weight | Formula | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CMA Adjusted Sales ($V_{\text{cma}}$)** | $0.60$ | $V_{\text{cma}} = \frac{\sum_{k} S_k \cdot \text{AdjustedPrice}_k}{\sum_k S_k}$ | Similarity-weighted mean of non-outlier adjusted comparable prices. |
| **Submarket Trend Index ($V_{\text{trend}}$)** | $0.15$ | $V_{\text{trend}} = A_{\text{subj}} \times \text{MedianPSF}_{\text{latest}}$ | Subject square footage multiplied by the latest submarket sales PSF index. |
| **Location & Quality Factor ($V_{\text{loc}}$)** | $0.10$ | $V_{\text{loc}} = V_{\text{cma}} \times (1.0 + \Delta_{\text{cond}} + \Delta_{\text{amen}})$ | Subject condition and amenity premium applied to base comp baseline. |
| **Income Capitalization ($V_{\text{yield}}$)** | $0.15$ | $V_{\text{yield}} = \frac{12 \times R_{\text{mkt}}}{Y_{\text{gross}}}$ | Annualized market rent capitalized by the submarket gross capitalization yield ($Y_{\text{gross}}$). |

### Dynamic Weight Renormalization
If operational rent-roll or yield data is missing ($V_{\text{yield}} = \text{None}$):
$$w_{\text{yield}} = 0.0$$
The remaining weights are renormalized dynamically so their sum equals $1.00$:
$$w'_j = \frac{w_j}{w_{\text{cma}} + w_{\text{trend}} + w_{\text{loc}}}$$
- Renormalized baseline: $w'_{\text{cma}} = 70.6\%$, $w'_{\text{trend}} = 17.6\%$, $w'_{\text{loc}} = 11.8\%$.

### Valuation Range Bounds
To establish conservative institutional boundaries:
$$\text{Spread} = \max\left(0.04, \min\left(0.10, \frac{\text{StdDev}(P)}{\text{Mean}(P)}\right)\right)$$
$$V_{\text{low}} = V_{\text{est}} \times (1.0 - \text{Spread})$$
$$V_{\text{high}} = V_{\text{est}} \times (1.0 + \text{Spread})$$

## 5. Dynamic Rental Pricing Engine

### Purpose
Calculates a 3-tier optimal monthly rental pricing band `[Floor, Midpoint, Ceiling]` tailored to operational occupancy leverage, lease expiration cliffs, and short-term submarket momentum.

### Pricing Band Formulations

#### 1. Recommended Midpoint Rent ($R_{\text{mid}}$)
$$R_{\text{mid}} = R_{\text{base}} \times (1.0 + \delta_{\text{occ}} + \delta_{\text{cliff}} + \delta_{\text{cond}} + \delta_{\text{mom}})$$

Where:
- $R_{\text{base}}$: Base market rent from rental comps adjusted for subject sqft.
- $\delta_{\text{occ}}$: **Occupancy Leverage Adjustment**:
  $$\delta_{\text{occ}} = \begin{cases} +0.035 & \text{if } \text{Occupancy} \ge 95\% \\ 0.000 & \text{if } 85\% \le \text{Occupancy} < 95\% \\ -0.040 & \text{if } \text{Occupancy} < 85\% \end{cases}$$
- $\delta_{\text{cliff}}$: **Lease Expiration Timing Adjustment**:
  $$\delta_{\text{cliff}} = \begin{cases} -0.025 & \text{if } \text{Expiring}_{60\text{d}} \ge 30\% \text{ of units} \\ 0.000 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
- $\delta_{\text{cond}}$: Condition rating adjustment ($\pm 0.02$ per condition tier difference from Good).
- $\delta_{\text{mom}}$: 6-month submarket price momentum factor ($\frac{\text{Momentum}_{\text{6m}}}{2}$).

#### 2. Defensive Floor Rent ($R_{\text{floor}}$)
$$R_{\text{floor}} = R_{\text{mid}} \times 0.92$$
*(Defensive, high-velocity pricing for accelerated absorption).*

#### 3. Yield-Maximizing Ceiling Rent ($R_{\text{ceiling}}$)
$$R_{\text{ceiling}} = R_{\text{mid}} \times 1.08$$
*(Target rate during peak leasing seasons or low-vacancy environments).*

#### 4. In-Place Rent Gap Analysis
$$\text{RentGap}_{\text{amt}} = R_{\text{mid}} - R_{\text{in-place}}$$
$$\text{RentGap}_{\text{pct}} = \frac{\text{RentGap}_{\text{amt}}}{R_{\text{in-place}}} \times 100\%$$

## 6. Operational Rent Roll & Expiration Cliff Ladder

### Occupancy & Vacancy Metrics
$$\text{Physical Occupancy Rate} = \frac{\text{Occupied Units}}{\text{Total Units}}$$
$$\text{Physical Vacancy Rate} = 1.0 - \text{Physical Occupancy Rate}$$
$$\text{Economic Occupancy Rate} = \frac{\text{Current In-Place Monthly Rent}}{\text{Gross Potential Monthly Rent}}$$
$$\text{Economic Vacancy Rate} = 1.0 - \text{Economic Occupancy Rate}$$

### 30 / 60 / 90-Day Lease Expiration Cliff Ladder
Computes the count and cumulative percentage of in-place leases expiring within rolling 30-day windows:
$$E_{30} = \sum \mathbb{I}(\text{DaysUntilExpiration} \le 30)$$
$$E_{60} = \sum \mathbb{I}(31 \le \text{DaysUntilExpiration} \le 60)$$
$$E_{90} = \sum \mathbb{I}(61 \le \text{DaysUntilExpiration} \le 90)$$
$$E_{>90} = \sum \mathbb{I}(\text{DaysUntilExpiration} > 90)$$

$$\text{Lease Turnover Exposure \%} = \frac{E_{30} + E_{60} + E_{90}}{\text{Total Units}} \times 100\%$$

## 7. Submarket Trends & Momentum Engine

### Annualized Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)
Computed over a 24-month horizon between the earliest period ($t_0$) and latest period ($t_m$):

$$\text{CAGR} = \left(\frac{P_{t_m}}{P_{t_0}}\right)^{\frac{12}{m}} - 1.0$$
- $P$: Median sales price or monthly rental rate.
- $m$: Number of months in historical window (typically $24$).

### 6-Month Trailing Price Momentum
$$\text{Momentum}_{\text{6m}} = \frac{P_{\text{latest}} - P_{\text{latest}-6}}{P_{\text{latest}-6}} \times 100\%$$

### Gross Capitalization Yield
$$Y_{\text{gross}} = \frac{12 \times \text{MedianRentPSF}}{\text{MedianSalePSF}} \times 100\%$$

## 8. Multi-Factor Deterministic Confidence Scoring

The confidence score $C \in [0.0, 100.0]$ is computed as the sum of five independent, objective analytical factors (each weighted at max 20 points):

$$C = F_{\text{quantity}} + F_{\text{dispersion}} + F_{\text{freshness}} + F_{\text{market}} + F_{\text{lease}}$$

### Scoring Dimension Breakdown

| Factor | Max Points | Evaluation Logic | Deterministic Points Awarded |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Comp Quantity & Similarity** | 20 pts | $\ge 4$ comps with avg similarity $\ge 0.80$<br>$3$ comps or avg similarity $\ge 0.70$<br>$< 3$ comps | $20\text{ pts}$<br>$14\text{ pts}$<br>$6\text{ pts}$ |
| **2. Comp Price Dispersion** | 20 pts | $\text{Coefficient of Variation (CV)} < 8\%$<br>$8\% \le \text{CV} \le 15\%$<br>$\text{CV} > 15\%$ | $20\text{ pts}$<br>$12\text{ pts}$<br>$5\text{ pts}$ |
| **3. Transaction Freshness** | 20 pts | Most recent comp $< 90\text{ days}$<br>Most recent comp $90 - 180\text{ days}$<br>Most recent comp $> 180\text{ days}$ | $20\text{ pts}$<br>$13\text{ pts}$<br>$5\text{ pts}$ |
| **4. Submarket Trend Stability** | 20 pts | $\ge 24\text{ months of trend data with steady momentum}$<br>$12 - 23\text{ months of data}$<br>Missing or high volatility trend data | $20\text{ pts}$<br>$12\text{ pts}$<br>$5\text{ pts}$ |
| **5. Lease Roll Operational Data** | 20 pts | Active rent roll present, occupancy $\ge 90\%$, rollover $< 30\%$<br>Rent roll present with high vacancy/rollover<br>No rent roll data provided | $20\text{ pts}$<br>$12\text{ pts}$<br>$6\text{ pts}$ |

### Confidence Tier Classification
- **`HIGH`**: $C \ge 80.0$ points.
- **`MEDIUM`**: $60.0 \le C < 80.0$ points.
- **`LOW`**: $C < 60.0$ points.

## 9. Risk & Data Quality Auditing

The system evaluates eight distinct risk and data quality rules, deducting points from a perfect score of 100:

$$\text{DataQualityScore} = \max\left(0.0, 100.0 - \sum \text{Penalties}\right)$$

| Audit Rule Category | Trigger Condition | Severity | Penalty |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `INSUFFICIENT_COMPS` | Selected comps count $< 3$ | `CRITICAL` | 25 pts |
| `HIGH_DISPERSION` | Comps adjusted price CV $> 15\%$ | `HIGH` | 15 pts |
| `STALE_DATA` | Oldest comp closed $> 180$ days ago | `MEDIUM` | 10 pts |
| `HIGH_NET_ADJUSTMENTS` | Comp net adjustments $> 25\%$ of sale price | `HIGH` | 15 pts |
| `LEASE_EXPIRATION_CLIFF` | Expirations within 90 days $> 35\%$ of units | `HIGH` | 15 pts |
| `HIGH_VACANCY` | Physical occupancy rate $< 85\%$ | `HIGH` | 15 pts |
| `RENT_GAP_DISCREPANCY` | Current in-place rent $> 20\%$ below market | `MEDIUM` | 10 pts |
| `MISSING_DATA` | Missing rent roll or submarket trend fixture | `MEDIUM` | 10 pts |

### Overall Risk Severity Determination
- **`CRITICAL`**: Any critical severity flag triggered or $\text{DataQualityScore} < 50.0$.
- **`HIGH`**: Any high severity flag triggered or $\text{DataQualityScore} < 70.0$.
- **`MEDIUM`**: Medium severity flags present.
- **`LOW`**: Zero high/critical flags and $\text{DataQualityScore} \ge 85.0$.
